# RTX 5090: completed optimization findings

This is the final local GB202 pass. Earlier CPU-versus-GPU and A100/RTX 3090
results remain separate comparisons; none are multiplied into these ratios.

## Measured throughput and identity

Three frozen boundaries, P1/P3, and two repeats per arm were executed using
the strict SM120 packed solver. The retained resident and backend have hash
prefixes `60d248` and `2eb80b` respectively. Independent processes used one CPU
affinity per worker and `max_threads=1` for every unchanged VMEC input.

| Repeat | Serial six-case suite s | Three-process batch s | Throughput ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 76.0001732 | 46.9197576 | 1.6197904x |
| 1 | 77.2482494 | 46.9669819 | 1.6447352x |

All times are measured Windows QPC marker-receipt intervals; ratios are
arithmetic derived from executed runs. The scope includes cold processes,
metric work, output persistence, scientific checks and owned retirement.
IPC jitter remains in the measurement. Two repeats do not establish a
confidence interval or production accounting benefit.

The 24 untraced calls passed every original metric, request/deck, physical-array,
NetCDF and repeat gate. All 276 numeric metric observations and twelve nulls
matched exactly. A separate twelve-call traced qualification also passed:
138 numeric observations, six unchanged nulls and exact physical repeats.
Maximum absolute and relative metric drift is zero for every reported numeric
metric. Traced qualification timings are excluded from performance claims.

The [six-case table](gb202/results/benchmark-six-cases.csv) reports individual
serial and loaded-batch latency separately. Its Linux RAW medians are diagnostic,
not the primary speedup clock. Loaded individual VMEC latency increased by
2.32-2.79x. See [all case/metric drift rows](gb202/results/metric-drift-by-case.csv),
[metric values](gb202/results/metric-values.csv), and
[per-metric maxima](gb202/results/metric-maxima.csv).

## Profile-driven experiments

The inverse executes 12,208,224 strict DADD/DMUL operations, with no DFMA.
Its operation rate normalized by measured average SM cycles is 83.11% of the
executed strict-arithmetic reference, versus approximately 60% of the hardware
FP64-pipe denominator. This is a kernel-local empirical comparison, not a
universal ceiling or whole-solver roofline result. The reference includes its
checksum work; cycle normalization does not remove instruction-mix or memory
differences. See [the derived calculation](gb202/results/empirical-roofline.json).

The full inverse counter capture showed 99.89% L2 hit rate. Reducing inverse
registers from 126 to 95 doubled active warps but barely changed elapsed GPU
cycles. Removing runtime indexing reciprocals also produced no gain.
Splitting independent forward recurrences improved the component by about
1.06x; whole-solver pairs were mixed, 1.0134x and 0.9931x. Explicit cache-global
loads were slower in every measured pair. These candidates were not promoted.
The [candidate table](gb202/results/candidate-table.csv) retains their outcomes.
The [kernel counter table](gb202/results/kernel-counters.csv) retains the
separate executed captures. Frequencies and caches were unlocked: compare
their cycles cautiously, not unmatched-clock durations. Reported cache hit
values above 100% are profiler counter artifacts, not physical hit probabilities.

No repeatable additional single-solve speedup was established in this pass.
The demonstrated improvement is independent-solve throughput.

## Build and operational scope

The [source snapshot and build recipe](gb202/README.md) preserve the measured
numerical translation units and strict compiler flags. This is an experimental
backend configuration, not automatic activation in the default Python solver.
It needs CUDA and the pinned VMEC++ 0.4.11 reference environment for integration.
Never substitute a different reference extension and retain an old parity claim.

Keep precision, fidelity, modes, tolerances, iteration limits, multigrid schedule,
`nstep` and single-thread VMEC unchanged. Batch independent requests with separate
outputs; integrate cache/request identity, owned timeouts and actual metering
before production adoption. Higher throughput can increase individual elapsed
charges. A two-thread shared-context screen passed physical checks but did not
establish an advantage over process batching. Private MPS failed before client
startup; owned services retired, and its precise startup failure is unresolved.

Early single-fixture screens were CPU-contended and are exploratory. That CPU
workload ended before the final mixed-fixture campaign. The local GPU was
released afterward. No new cloud lease, physics change, benchmark-original edit,
submission change or production scheduler change was introduced.

Raw profiles, binary builds, graphs and full scientific outputs remain ignored
under `build/optimization`. Only source and aggregate result tables are versioned.
